The white paper *The Oracle and the Partner* is now public, in the text of version 3.1, together with a tracker for the oracles it proposes.

An oracle, as the paper defines it, is a way to score a proposed solution without a person in the loop: quickly, cheaply, and with some fidelity to the real world. A compiler is an oracle for code. A held-out test set can be an oracle for a model. Most of the problems that matter for African prosperity do not have one. A guess about a road, a minigrid, a clinic, or a crop can sit untested for a season or a decade. Where there is no score, there is nothing for a research system, human or automated, to improve against.

The paper’s inversion is to build the score first. Models and agents are improving every month, and much of that improvement is published. A benchmark for informal cross-border trade, or a freight twin that can be checked against real consignments, does not go out of date in the same way. If it is public, other people’s work can be measured against it. If it is never specified, it does not exist: nobody outside the problem will specify it for us.

This site is the public record of that project. It is not a fund, a secretariat, or a laboratory. At launch the tracker holds fifty-two proposed entries. Forty-six are the benchmark families in Annex A. Six are infrastructure the paper names separately: a freight and trade digital twin, three laboratories, an agricultural trial network, and African language corpora. No lead is named, because none has been appointed. No milestone date is shown, because none has been set. Dataset licences read “Unassigned” until a dataset exists and a licence is chosen.

The paper itself is the argument: which partners, which domains, which institutions, and where the method does not apply. Governance and poverty are treated as central and, at the same time, as unfit for this kind of search, because they have no ground truth. That limit is part of the claim.

Corrections are welcome, and they should name a section. A figure should be moved only with a primary source. New oracles can be proposed. The tracker will not present an entry as specified, or as live, until the file says so and the evidence is linked.
