# Version history and errata

The reading text on this site is the verified master of *The Oracle and the Partner*, whose own header reads **Version 3.1 — July 2026**. That file is the text dated 8 August 2026. It is the only full text published here.

## What changed from 3.0 to 3.1

The corrections do not replace the paper’s argument. They change how four claims were sourced, restate a fifth, and fix the version header. Figures below are the corrected ones, as they stand in the reading text. Annex C is the place to check a number against its source.

| | Where it appears | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Section 1.2, and the falsification note in section 12.1 | A narrowing of the open-weight gap is attributed to the UK AI Security Institute’s cyber measure (four to seven months, from six to ten). Epoch AI’s composite is reported as an average lag of four months over January–May 2026, marginally wider than the three-month average Epoch had measured to October 2025. Version 3.0 had cited Epoch for a narrowing Epoch’s own comparison does not show. |
| 2 | Sections 1.1 and 9.1, the conclusion, Annex C, and the glossary | Modular Open-Source Identity Platform engagement is stated as about sixteen of twenty-nine countries in Africa, with thirteen national rollouts under way. Version 3.0 said nine of eleven implementing countries, from an older secondary count. |
| 3 | The passages on PAPSS, including sections 19.1 and 23 and Annex C | The Pan-African Payment and Settlement System is described as operational since 2022, with savings on the order of 27 percent against correspondent banking, and as connecting nineteen of fifty-four countries. Version 3.0 had dated entry into force to 2025 and omitted the coverage gap. |
| 4 | The Gulf capital passages, including sections 8 and 16, section 27, and Annexes B and C | Worldwide sovereign-wealth deployment into AI and digitalisation in 2025 is stated as $66 billion. Gulf funds are described as about 43 percent of global sovereign-wealth capital, with at least $23 billion of identifiable deployment. Version 3.0 had treated the $66 billion as a Gulf-only figure. |
| 5 | The United Arab Emirates investment passage and Annex C | Investment into Africa is stated as exceeding $110 billion between 2019 and 2024. Version 3.0 had said $118 billion for 2020–2024. |
| 6 | The document header | The version is numbered 3.1. |

A further clarification, not a retraction: the finding that 83 percent of early Continental AI Strategy funding sat in four countries is unchanged, and version 3.1 names the four — Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa, and Egypt.

## How to propose a correction

Use the “Suggest a correction” link on the section. It opens a GitHub issue with the section name filled in. If you do not have a GitHub account, use the [contact form](https://oraquul.github.io/AI-Africa-Abundance/contribute/#contact) and name the section.

A correction to a figure should cite the primary publisher. The editor does not rewrite a published version in place. A further version, if one is issued, will be added to this table, and the file it replaces will be left as it was.
